Classic Origins

RE1

Resident Evil (1996)

Story context: Established fixed-camera survival mechanics, item friction and room-based tension before the series shifted to modern controls.

Why include: This is the oldest playable anchor. It defines what players still expect from survival horror rhythm in later remakes.

Origin: RE1 defined the franchise’s core architecture and became the oldest benchmark before every later gameplay conversion.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Gameplay walk-through (search)

Playable traits: Classic baseline with deliberate movement and strict inventory management, useful for understanding original survival pressure.

RE2

Resident Evil 2 (1998)

Story context: Extends the franchise with Claire and Leon in parallel arcs, and a wider crisis after the Raccoon City incident.

Why include: Most useful legacy baseline for inventory rhythm and campaign urgency before modern remakes.

Origin: RE2 preserved the original survival loop while scaling scope, making it a direct historical comparator for modern tuning.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Gameplay walk-through (search)

Playable traits: Sharper room pressure and clearer combat-readability than RE1, with stronger checkpoint cadence.

RE3

Resident Evil 3: Nemesis (1999)

Story context: Introduces a recurring threat pressure model centered around the Nemesis chase and escape rhythm.

Why include: Important to understand how threat persistence changed pacing before modern action-remake design.

Origin: RE3 formalized repetitive pursuit pressure, which later games and remakes later borrow for intensity calibration.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Nemesis pressure gameplay

Playable traits: Higher urgency profile with timed pressure from repeating boss pressure and tighter encounter resets.

RE CV

Resident Evil Code: Veronica (2000)

Story context: Direct narrative predecessor for Veronica, extending the Claire/Chris arc through broader island and travel settings.

Why include: The most relevant non-remake anchor because Veronica inherits setting and character context from this node.

Origin: Code Veronica is the direct predecessor chain, so relation mapping for Veronica starts here before any modernization.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Story and atmosphere

Playable traits: Longer transitions and sustained movement segments, while still grounded in classic survival rhythm.

RE0

Resident Evil 0 (2002)

Story context: Prequel branch with Rebecca and Billy, using a companion-first puzzle and combat structure before Veronica’s broader arc.

Why include: Useful if visitors want the predecessor style before full cinematic action transitions.

Origin: RE0 preserves early design rhythm with partner framing and helps explain how the series handled tension before later remasters.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Co-op style moments (search)

Playable traits: Twin-role pacing and checkpoint placement emphasize sequencing and short-term strategy.

RE4

Resident Evil 4 (2005)

Story context: Moves the series into more cinematic action-survival using Leon in a wider mission geography.

Why include: This is the structural pivot before RE4 Remake shows the full modernization path.

Origin: RE4 introduced the major shift toward modern action timing, a key reason RE4 Remake is in the playable comparison queue.

Official references: Steam listing search

Clips: RE4 gameplay search

Playable traits: Stronger movement and enemy density profile than earlier entries while keeping survival constraints in place.

RE5

Resident Evil 5 (2009)

Story context: Expands the story into broader action and cooperative structures with stronger mission throughput.

Why include: Important contrast for visitors asking why some Veronica claims feel more restrained than action-era entries.

Origin: RE5 illustrates a short period where action tempo outpaced narrow survival structure; valuable for direction clarity.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Action benchmark (search)

Playable traits: Larger combat spaces and faster flow, useful for understanding modern action-pressure branches.

RE6

Resident Evil 6 (2012)

Story context: Splits into multiple protagonists and campaign branches, increasing structural complexity.

Why include: A cautionary comparison for scope and pacing: useful when discussing what Veronica likely did not inherit.

Origin: RE6 showed how broad campaign design can dilute intensity; it helps position why remakes moved back toward tighter control.

Official references: Capcom RE hub / Steam listing search

Clips: Campaign structure search

Playable traits: High throughput with heavier sequence density and mission segmentation.

RE7

Resident Evil 7: Biohazard (2017)

Story context: Re-centers the series around first-person dread and tighter house-scale immersion.

Why include: Critical modern baseline before RE8 and before current remake expectations in narrative tone.

Origin: RE7 introduced the perspective reset that made later releases feel more intimate and immediate in threat reaction.

Official references: Steam / Capcom RE hub

Clips: Official trailer

Playable traits: Close-quarters exploration and fear rhythm become stronger than classical static-room sequencing.

RE2 Remake

Resident Evil 2 Remake (2019)

Story context: Modern RE2 rebuild with near-complete system refresh on a proven chapter skeleton.

Why include: Most reliable modern anchor for how a legacy chapter is remade for current audience habits.

Origin: RE2 Remake is the current proof point for Capcom’s baseline conversion approach, directly useful for Veronica expectation framing.

Official references: Steam

Clips: Official trailer

Playable traits: Clearer input responsiveness, improved movement pacing and preserved inventory pressure under modern control systems.

RE3 Remake

Resident Evil 3 Remake (2020)

Story context: Rebuild of RE3 with compressed pacing and modern threat readability.

Why include: Supports players comparing campaign density and urgency before deciding on Veronica expectations.

Origin: RE3 Remake demonstrates what a short remaster-like conversion can preserve while changing input and timing conventions.

Official references: Steam

Clips: Official trailer

Playable traits: Faster loop compression and clearer enemy telegraphing for modern players without full structural rewrite.

RE8

Resident Evil Village (2021)

Story context: Post-RE7 continuation with stronger boss-driven progression and broader modern traversal.

Why include: Current mainstream reference for players comparing how modern scale and pacing may continue into Veronica.

Origin: RE8 marks the recent benchmark before Veronica and helps place expectations for atmosphere versus combat density.

Official references: Steam

Clips: Official trailer

Playable traits: Large modern action-horror map language with tactical movement and boss rhythm.

RE4 Remake

Resident Evil 4 Remake (2023)

Story context: Current modernized form of RE4 with full contemporary control and encounter systems.

Why include: Most direct modern template for expected gameplay translation quality and tone transfer.

Origin: RE4 Remake is the nearest official modern reference for how Capcom remasters legacy structure at 2023-level polish.

Official references: Steam

Clips: Official trailer

Playable traits: Tighter combat windows, revised pacing and stronger player agency under legacy camera legacy converted to modern expectations.
