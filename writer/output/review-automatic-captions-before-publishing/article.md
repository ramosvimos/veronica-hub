# How do you review automatic captions before publishing a Reel?

Published: 2026-10-09

Review automatic captions against the final video, checking the words first and the viewing experience second. A generated subtitle file is a starting point. It does not establish that every word is correct, that the timings match your edited cut, or that the captions communicate all audio information your audience needs.

## Decide what a finished automatic caption review means

Write a short acceptance note before you begin: the final captions should reflect the intended recording, remain understandable during playback, and contain no unresolved guesses presented as certain words. This keeps a review from becoming an endless cycle of cosmetic tweaks while substantive errors remain.

W3C’s caption guidance explains that accessible captions include speech and relevant non-speech audio and that automatic captions need verification. That is broader than obtaining a transcript of spoken words. Video Script Extractor provides a speech-transcription starting point; its official review guide also says it does not assign speaker labels. Plan any additional caption editing in your chosen editor rather than assuming it happened automatically.

## Review meaning before punctuation

Read one short passage while listening to the corresponding audio. Ask whether the sentence communicates the same proposition, not merely whether it looks grammatical. In particular, flag a sentence that changes certainty, reverses a condition, or turns a possibility into an instruction. A beautifully punctuated sentence can still convey the wrong message.

Try an illustrative review exercise: a speaker explains that a setting is optional for this demonstration. If the draft captions say the setting is required, the error matters more than a missing comma. Correct it from the recording and inspect the surrounding explanation. Do not generalize from this fictional example into a claim about how often the product makes that mistake.

## Keep an unresolved list while you work

Use a separate list for passages that interrupt the review. Record the time, the current text, and the exact uncertainty. “Is this the product name or an ordinary word?” is a useful question. “Bad caption” is less useful because a second reviewer must reconstruct what worried you. Continue reviewing other passages if you can do so without relying on the unresolved wording.

Assign responsibility for the list before publication. A creator may know a product’s proper name, while a fluent speaker may be needed for a passage in another language. If nobody can verify a phrase, choose an honest treatment appropriate to the content rather than filling the gap with a guess. W3C’s transcription guidance discusses identifying unintelligible speech and preserving the meaning of what was actually said.

## Check timing using the exact video you will publish

Freeze a review copy of the intended final cut, then import the captions into that project. Watch transitions between speech and silence, changes in speaker, and any point where an edit moves the recording forward. A caption should not remain on screen as if it belongs to a different sentence or a different person.

Do a second viewing focused on the experience of reading. Ask whether each cue stays long enough to follow, whether a break divides an awkward phrase, and whether the next caption arrives before you have processed the previous one. These are editorial review questions, not universal numerical limits. Follow any concrete requirements of your destination and check their current documentation when a project depends on them.

## Review presentation in the destination player

Preview the published or staged version at the size your audience will use. Text that looked comfortable on an editing monitor may feel crowded in a phone-sized view. Check whether captions cover a relevant label, whether important text is clipped, and whether opening player controls obscures something the viewer needs. Change the layout where the destination gives you that control.

Also check that the delivered track is the intended revision. A common process failure is approving an edited file but uploading its earlier draft. Give the approved file a distinct name and include it in the final delivery checklist. If captions are permanently embedded into the picture, preview the rendered video because the editable project alone is not the delivered artifact.

1. Play the final media with the selected caption track enabled.
2. Check the beginning, end, and every edited transition.
3. Watch a representative section at a small viewing size.
4. Confirm that the uploaded or rendered result uses the approved revision.

## Record review scope without inventing an accuracy score

Write down the media filename, caption filename, review date, reviewer, and any open issues. You can say that the full recording was checked when that is what happened. Avoid turning an informal review into a percentage or a claim that the service is always accurate. A meaningful measured accuracy result requires a defined reference and a documented method.

For collaborative work, make a clear decision at the end: approved, approved after specific changes, or needs another review. Include the exact changes in the second case. “Looks fine” leaves too much room for a teammate to assume that wording, timing, speaker identification, and accessibility were all checked when only one of those tasks was done.

## Conclusion

An automatic caption review is complete when the actual delivery version has been checked and any remaining uncertainty is handled openly. Separate wording, timing, and presentation passes so none disappears inside a vague approval. Keep the reviewed files together and verify the version your audience will see.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a successful subtitle import mean the captions are ready?

No. It shows that the destination accepted the file. Review the wording, timing, and playback separately before publication.

### Should captions identify important sounds as well as speech?

When sounds are needed to understand the content, caption guidance calls for representing that information. A speech-only draft may need additional work.

### Can I claim perfect accuracy after reviewing one section?

No. A checked section establishes only what you examined there. Describe your actual review scope and avoid unsupported performance claims.
