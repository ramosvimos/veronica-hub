How the series changed

A quick timeline of the games most relevant to Veronica.

1996

Resident Evil

Established the series' mix of limited supplies, puzzles and slow-building tension.

Why it matters: Set the basic survival-horror structure that later games kept changing.

The starting point for comparing every later entry.

1998

Resident Evil 2

Expanded the story with Claire and Leon's parallel routes through Raccoon City.

Why it matters: Made exploration broader while keeping inventory pressure and puzzle-heavy progression.

Especially relevant because Claire returns in Veronica.

1999

Resident Evil 3: Nemesis

Made a recurring pursuer central to the campaign.

Why it matters: Added a faster, more relentless kind of tension.

Useful context for how the series handles chase sequences.

2000

Resident Evil Code: Veronica

Continued Claire's search for Chris beyond Raccoon City.

Why it matters: Introduced Rockfort Island and the story now being remade.

The direct source material for Resident Evil Veronica.

2002

Resident Evil 0

Added character switching and partner-based puzzles.

Why it matters: Pushed the classic fixed-camera formula toward more complex character management.

Related design context rather than part of Veronica's direct story.

2005

Resident Evil 4

Moved the series to an over-the-shoulder camera and faster combat.

Why it matters: Changed how Resident Evil handles movement, aiming and enemy encounters.

The turning point behind the controls used by the modern remakes.

2009

Resident Evil 5

Expanded the action focus with co-op and larger combat spaces.

Why it matters: Moved further away from the slower isolation of the early games.

Useful mainly as a contrast with the remake era.

2012

Resident Evil 6

Split the game across several protagonists and campaigns.

Why it matters: Took the series' action-heavy direction to its widest scale.

Helps explain why later games returned to tighter settings.

2017

Resident Evil 7: Biohazard

Returned the focus to horror through a first-person camera and a smaller setting.

Why it matters: Made close-range exploration and vulnerability central again.

The beginning of the current RE Engine era.

2019

Resident Evil 2 Remake

Rebuilt a fixed-camera classic as a modern over-the-shoulder game.

Why it matters: Showed how Capcom can change controls and level flow without losing the original atmosphere.

The clearest modern comparison for a Code: Veronica remake.

2020

Resident Evil 3 Remake

Condensed the original into a shorter, faster remake.

Why it matters: Showed that Capcom may change campaign length and structure, not just presentation.

Relevant when considering how much of Code: Veronica may be reworked.

2021

Resident Evil Village

Combined first-person horror with broader areas and more action-heavy bosses.

Why it matters: Shows the larger scale the RE Engine games can support.

A useful contrast with the tighter recent remakes.

2023

Resident Evil 4 Remake

Rebuilt another classic around modern controls, parrying and expanded encounters.

Why it matters: Shows the current standard for changing combat while preserving a familiar story and tone.

The newest completed remake to compare with Veronica.

2027

Resident Evil Veronica Remake

Capcom confirms the remake for 2027 on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Switch 2 and PC.

Why it matters: The question is no longer whether the remake exists, but what Capcom will change.

Exact date remains unconfirmed.
